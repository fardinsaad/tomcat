CP versus Fs-CoT
Both variants share an identical skeleton — task framing, objectives, rules, the live grid, and the query. They diverge only in what fills the demonstration slot, and Fs-CoT adds four extra rules. Everything else is held constant.

Seven labelled exemplars, no figures Each one is a complete input → output trace. Human Action: The human moves downward from their current position at (6,4) to (8,4), where they provide the instruction. Instruction: Can you pass me the blue key? Type: Unclear. Based on the human's movement, instruction and the current grid configuration, I infer that the human moved to (8,4) to collect the gem at (8,9) located beyond the blue door and red door at (8,6) and (8,8)… Since the human does not mention passing the red key, it is unclear. Response: …So I will collect one blue key and one red key located at (3,8) and (5,8). To minimize movement, I will first pick up the blue key at (3,8) followed by the red key at (5,8)… Actions: 1) Collect: blue_key at (3,8). 2) Collect: red_key at (5,8). 3) Pass: blue_key and red_key to the human at (8,4). 4) Unlock: human unlocks the Blue_door at (8,6) and the Red_door at (8,8). 5) Retrieve: human retrieves gem at (8,9). … ×6 more, mixing clear and unclear cases, key-requests and door-requests.

The asymmetry is the point. CP supplies commonsense knowledge — grids rendered as figures with prose explaining how the domain behaves — but never shows a labelled answer. Fs-CoT supplies seven fully worked Type → Response → Actions traces but no figures. Same task, same rules, same grid: what changes is whether the model is told how the world works or shown what a good answer looks like.
